The highest-pressure balls in the data

One per match, ranked by Pressure Index. Win probability of the batting side before → after the ball.

Method in brief: pressure, baseline, rating
  • Win probability. Chase: MLP on runs needed, balls left, wickets in hand, required rate and IPL flag. First innings: projected runs still to come (Poisson MLP with negative-binomial spread) averaged over the chase-start win probability, so WP is continuous across the innings break.
  • Pressure Index. Leverage = Σ p(outcome) × |ΔWP| over 0, 1, 2, 4, 6, wicket and wide, with outcome probabilities shifted by collapse, dot squeeze, new batter and bowler-just-hit (all significant after controlling for leverage). Normalised within each innings, × (1 + 0.25 × high stakes). High+ = top 20%.
  • Fair baseline. Out-of-fold gradient-boosted models predict runs and dismissals for an average player in the same situation against the same opponent quality (empirical-Bayes shrunk). Wickets are valued in runs with a Duckworth–Lewis-style resource curve.
  • Rating. Level = leverage-weighted runs above expected per 100 High+ balls; Delta = Level − own Low/Medium level; Consistency = shrunk share of pressure matches with positive value plus the 25th-percentile match; Impact = High+ win-probability added per pressure match. All rates are empirical-Bayes shrunk.
  • Limits. Thin samples, no ICC World Cups, associate opposition adjusted only through opponent quality, no fielding, and first-innings collapses rate only as moderate pressure under any win-probability measure.
